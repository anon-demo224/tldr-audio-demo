TLDR:

Temporal Downsampling and Reconstruction for Accelerating Pretrained Autoregressive Text-to-Speech Models


Anonymous Submission

Paper under double-blind review

Abstract. Codec-based autoregressive text-to-speech (AR-TTS) models achieve strong synthesis performance through large-scale pretraining, but their token-level decoding incurs substantial computation and KV-cache costs. Patch-based autoregressive modeling shortens the decoding sequence by grouping consecutive speech tokens; however, existing approaches are typically designed and trained specifically for patch-level generation and use a fixed patch size, limiting both pretrained-model adaptation and deployment flexibility. We introduce TemporaL Downsampling and Reconstruction (TLDR), a post-training acceleration framework that adapts pretrained AR-TTS models. TLDR combines a token-to-patch encoder with a patch-to-token decoder, reducing the sequence processed by the global backbone. Furthermore, we introduce a controllable variant with patch-size-specific LoRA adapters to control the decoding granularity within a single checkpoint. Experiments on three pretrained AR-TTS models show that, at k = 4, TLDR achieves 1.78×–2.49× inference speedups while maintaining word error rate comparable to that of the corresponding original models.

This page hosts audio samples only.

Overview

A conventional AR-TTS model emits one speech token per autoregressive step (A). TLDR inserts a token-to-patch encoder and a patch-to-token decoder around the frozen backbone (B), so the backbone runs once per patch of k tokens. The controllable variant keeps one adapter per patch size inside a single checkpoint, letting the decoding granularity — and with it the quality/speed operating point — be chosen at inference time.
